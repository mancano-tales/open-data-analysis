Published

September 10, 2026

Overview

This figure tracks the adjusted relative risk (RRA) of ever enrolling in tertiary education, for Brazilians aged 18-24, comparing the richest and poorest strata of the household per-capita income distribution — decile 10 versus decile 1 on the left, quintile 5 versus quintile 1 on the right — from 1992 to 2025. The RRA is the ratio between the model-adjusted probability of access for the top stratum and the bottom stratum, holding age, sex, race, region, and urban/rural location fixed across strata. It is an adapted replication of the REN (income) panel of Figure 3 in Salata, Bringhenti & Miranda (2025), Dados, which uses the same logit + adjusted-predictions design but a narrower population (18-24-year-olds still living with their parents) and stops in 2022.

Methodology

For each year, a logit model of ens_sup (ever enrolled in tertiary education) is fit on income decile/quintile plus age, sex, race, region, and urban/rural status, with survey weights. The RRA is the ratio of the average adjusted predicted probability for the top stratum over the bottom stratum — computed with marginaleffects::avg_predictions() and hypotheses(), the R equivalent of the Stata margins/nlcom pair the original article uses. Confidence intervals are computed via the delta method on the log scale (log(top) - log(bottom), then exponentiated): applying the delta method directly on the ratio scale produces implausible intervals — including negative bounds, impossible for a probability ratio — whenever the poorest stratum’s estimated access is very close to zero, which is common in the 1990s (e.g. the poorest decile’s raw access rate was 0.1% in 1992). Years without a usable PNAD/PNADC estimate (1994, 2000, 2010 — no survey; 2020-2021 — excluded for pandemic-era data-quality reasons) show as a break in the line, following the same convention as this repository’s Wagstaff index time series post; the underlying data file does carry a linearly interpolated value for those years (replicating footnote 21 of the original article), but it is not plotted.

Differences from Salata, Bringhenti & Miranda (2025). Two deliberate design choices make this an adapted replication rather than an exact one, and the RRA magnitude does not match the published Figure 3 point for point: (1) this figure uses the general population of 18-24-year-olds, not only those still living with their parents (the original article’s rec_mod restriction, needed there because the model also requires the household head’s own characteristics) — for consistency with every other figure in this repository’s PNAD/PNADC panel; (2) as a consequence, income decile/quintile cut points are computed over the general population rather than within that narrower subsample, which — especially in the 1990s, when the poorest stratum’s access was close to zero — can widen the gap further. Comparing against the article’s own reported values, the gap between this replication and the original is large in the noisy 1990s (roughly 3-7x), moderate around 2011 (roughly 2.5x, 12.3 here versus 5.0 in the article), and nearly closes by 2017-2022 (roughly 1.15-1.25x) — the ratio is not constant nor does it grow over time, it shrinks as both series approach the present.

Pipeline

This figure draws on the PNAD / PNAD Contínua Harmonization, 1992–2025 panel.

Scripts for this analysis, run in order:

  1. shared-pipeline/pnadc/035_Splice_Microdados.R — builds the harmonized panel, including income decile/quintile and the demographic controls used below.
  2. shared-pipeline/pnadc/037_Modelos_Logit_RRA_Renda.R — fits the 58 yearly logit models (decile and quintile) and computes the RRA with confidence intervals.
  3. Plotting: plot.R

Reproducibility

Tier: A

This pipeline uses only public data, accessible via package/API without any credential (PNADcIBGE). Run the scripts in shared-pipeline/pnadc/ in numeric order to reconstruct the intermediate data, then plot.R.
